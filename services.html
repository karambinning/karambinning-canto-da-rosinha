A ementa · The menu

What’s on the board, mostly.

A working sketch of what we cook. The board moves a little with what’s good that week — the easiest way to see what’s on today is to drop into the shop, or check Facebook for the daily specials.

Pastelaria Bakery

Pastel de nata
Custard tart, lightly scorched

The Portuguese custard tart, baked through the day. Crisp lamination, deep yolk, a strong bica on the side.

Bolo de arroz
Rice-flour muffin

A breakfast classic. Pair it with a galão before nine.

Bolos caseiros
Home-baked cakes

What’s on the counter today — lemon, chocolate, coconut, bolo de cenoura. Ask, or point.

Pão de queijo
Brazilian cheese bread

Warm from the oven. Quietly the best thing on the counter for some people.

Café Coffee

Bica · Espresso
Short, strong

The way the morning starts. Drink it standing if you’re in a hurry.

Galão
Espresso with hot milk, in a tall glass

Portuguese latte. The breakfast drink.

Meia de leite
Half coffee, half milk, in a cup

For the late morning, with a bolo on the side.

Cafezinho
Brazilian-style coffee

A small cup, served hot, sweetened to taste at the counter.

Da grelha From the grill

Frango de churrasco
Grilled Portuguese chicken

Properly butterflied, properly charred, with what it’s meant to be served with.

Bifana
Slow-cooked pork sandwich

A great-value lunch. The sandwich our regulars come back for.

Sandes de porco
Pork sandwich, served warm

Cooked slow, sliced thin, in a soft white roll. Mustard if you like.

Pratos do dia Today’s plate

Prato do dia
Today’s plate

There’s usually a hot plate on at lunchtime — cooked through the morning, on the counter from about midday until it’s gone. Ask what’s on, or peek at Facebook before you come.

Peixe
Fish

A small piece of fish, depending on the day — bacalhau when it’s on.

Petiscos
Small plates & nibbles

A few small things to pick at while you wait. What’s out is what’s on.

Mercearia The little grocery

Biscoitos & bolachas
Biscuits

The packets you grew up with. Bolacha Maria, Marie Lu, Cream-Cracker, the ones the supermarket here doesn’t carry.

Conservas
Tinned fish & preserves

A small shelf of sardines, tuna, lupini, the basics that make a Portuguese pantry.

Queijos & enchidos
Cheese & cured meat

What we have changes, but there’s usually a wedge or two and a few links worth taking home.

Bebidas & sumos
Drinks & juices

Sumol, Compal, the soft drinks that don’t make it onto the supermarket shelf.

Doces Something sweet

Bolo de cenoura
Brazilian carrot cake, chocolate top

When it’s on, get it.

Brigadeiros
Chocolate truffles

A small box for the kids’ birthday, or for the drive home.

Bolo do dia
Cake of the day

Whatever the kitchen baked this morning. Often the best slice of cake you’ll have all week.

Prices and prato-do-dia change with the week. The board on the wall is the canonical source — we’ve kept prices off the site on purpose so we’re not lying to you when ingredients move. Daily specials and what’s on the grill go up on our Facebook and Instagram.
