What we’re known for.

A short list, done properly. The pastry comes out of the oven the way it does in Lisbon. The grill is hot. The coffee is strong. You leave a little fuller and a little further from a Tuesday in Norfolk.

№ 01

Pastéis de nata

Pastel de nata

Crisp laminated shells, custard scorched on top, served warm with a strong bica on the side. The reason a lot of people walk in for the first time.

№ 02

Frango de churrasco

From the grill

Properly butterflied, properly charred, served with what it should be served with. The Portuguese way, not a tikka substitute.

№ 03

Bifana & pork sandwiches

Sandes de porco

The lunch our regulars come back for. Cooked slow, sliced thin, in a soft white roll. A great-value lunch for under a tenner.

№ 04

Café & bolos

Coffee & home-baked cakes

A small espresso, a galão, a bolo de arroz on the counter. The kind of stop that takes ten minutes and makes the afternoon different.

№ 05

A small mercearia

Home-comfort groceries

A shelf of the things you can’t find in the supermarket — biscuits, conservas, cheeses, the bits a Portuguese or Brazilian kitchen actually runs on.

№ 06

Family-run, casual

À mesa, devagar

No bookings, no fuss. Bring your phone, your mother-in-law, your kids. Sit down. Stay an hour. That’s how it works.

A note from the counter
— azulejo

Portuguese & Brazilian, in the middle of King’s Lynn.

Canto Da Rosinha is a small café tucked behind Lidl on Norfolk Street. We bake, we grill, we make the coffee the way it should be made, and we stock the small things from back home that the supermarket doesn’t.

On any given lunchtime you’ll hear three languages, see a baby in a pushchair next to a builder on his break, and a teacher reading the paper with a galão. That mix is the whole point.

We’re open most days. The hours move a little with the season, so the easiest way to know what we’re doing today is to peek at Facebook or just walk in. We’re here.

“The pastéis come out the way they do in Lisbon — proper.”
